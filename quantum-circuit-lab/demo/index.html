QUANTUM · a visual circuit lab

A qubit is a point on a sphere.

|0⟩ is the north pole and |1⟩ the south. As you scroll, a Hadamard gate turns the arrow from |0⟩ down to the equator. The arrow moves along the real path of the rotation (the state at every scroll position is Ht|0⟩), not along a blend of start and end.

SIMULATED Every result here comes from simulators running in your browser; the "hardware" is a mock backend.

Two qubits, one state.

H on q0, then a CNOT. During the CNOT both arrows shrink toward the centre: neither qubit has a state of its own any more. The violet arc between them is their entanglement (negativity), growing from 0 to 0.5 as the gate completes.

Measuring picks one answer.

This Bell pair gives 00 or 11, half the time each, never 01 or 10. One measurement collapses it to one of them; a histogram of 1,000 shots shows the distribution. In the lab you can do both, and add noise to see the never-outcomes appear.

Every qubit doubles the work.
