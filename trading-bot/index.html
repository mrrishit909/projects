Trading Bot

A paper-money trading system that scans stocks, options and crypto, checks every order against hard risk rules, and logs every decision for later grading.

PythonAlpaca APISQLiteClaude APIlaunchdhttp.server
Code on GitHub

Paper trading only: it uses Alpaca's pretend-money account. No real money is involved, and nothing here is investment advice.

The problem

I wanted to understand how an automated trading system is actually put together: data in, a decision rule, risk controls, execution, and a record you can audit afterwards. I also wanted to test a practical question: does an AI advisor make better calls than a simple rule? That needs a clean log of every decision, not just the trades.

What I built

A Python system that runs itself on a schedule against Alpaca's paper-trading API:

  • Strategy: a moving-average crossover (5-day vs 20-day) across about 70 large US stocks, plus options (long calls and puts only) and 24/7 crypto.
  • Risk engine: a "bouncer" that every order must pass: daily loss limit, trade-count cap, allowed-stock list, max positions, max dollars per stock.
  • Diary: SQLite tables for every run, decision (with its reasoning) and trade.
  • AI advisor: Claude reads prices, signals and news and gives an opinion per stock. It never trades; its calls are only logged and graded.
  • Dashboard: a local multi-page site: overview, holdings, options, crypto, decisions, trades, scoreboard.

How it was built, step by step

  1. 1

    Connect to the paper account

    Set up the project, stored the API keys in a .env file that is never committed, and printed the pretend account balance to prove the connection works.

  2. 2

    Look up prices, then buy and sell one share

    Fetched live quotes and daily bars, then placed a single market buy and sold it back. This was the smallest possible end-to-end test before any strategy existed.

  3. 3

    The safety bouncer (risk engine)

    Before writing any strategy, every order had to ask one function for permission. It answers YES or NO with a reason, and sells are always allowed so the bot can get out of a position.

    MAX_DOLLARS_PER_STOCK = 2000
MAX_STOCKS_HELD = 5
MAX_TRADES_PER_DAY = 16
DAILY_LOSS_LIMIT = 500

ok, why = can_i_trade(client, "AAPL", shares=3, price_per_share=230, side="buy")
# -> (False, "NO. We already hold 5 different stocks (limit is 5). Sell something first.")
  4. 4

    A first rule-based robot

    A moving-average crossover: buy when the 5-day average moves above the 20-day, sell when it drops back below. It is deliberately simple, so it works as a baseline.

  5. 5

    A diary of every move

    Three SQLite tables, runs, decisions and trades, record everything, including WAIT decisions and the reason for each one. Without this, there's nothing to grade later.

  6. 6

    Run by itself, plus a web dashboard

    auto_run.py loops during market hours; a local dashboard shows account value, holdings, decisions and trades. Later, macOS LaunchAgents replaced the loop so it runs unattended every 30 minutes for weeks.

  7. 7

    The AI advisor (advisory only)

    Claude Haiku reads each stock's recent prices, the crossover signal and news headlines, then answers BUY, SELL or WAIT with a reason. That answer goes into the diary as a separate strategy. It costs about a third of a cent per run.

  8. 8

    Scoreboard: rule vs AI

    compare.py lines up every decision against what the price actually did 3 or 5 trading days later, and checks whether either strategy beat simply staying invested.

  9. 9

    Scan a whole universe of stocks

    Instead of a handful of tickers, each run scans about 70 large, liquid US stocks, sells anything no longer trending up, and buys the strongest fresh crossovers, up to 5 positions.

  10. 10

    Options and crypto

    Options: buy a call on a fresh upward cross or a put on a downward one, one contract at a time. The bot closes at +50%, -50% or 5 days before expiry, and never sells options to open, so the worst case is what was paid. Crypto runs 24/7 on the same crossover idea, with its own smaller limits.

Results

  • A working, scheduled system that has paper-traded unattended, logging every run, decision and trade.
  • Every order passes through one auditable risk function, and the limits live in one settings file.
  • The rule-vs-AI scoreboard is in place; the comparison needs weeks of logged decisions before it means anything, so no win-rate is claimed here.

What I'd do next

  • Once enough history is logged, publish the scoreboard: hit rate and return for each strategy against buy-and-hold.
  • Backtest the crossover rule on several years of data before trusting the live numbers.
  • Replace fixed dollar sizing with volatility-based position sizing.
