AETHER · airport disruption recovery

A storm closes the runway. One model should not reschedule the airport.

90 movements, 1,797 connecting passengers, 30 gates and a ground fleet, on a synthetic night at Norrvik. Aircraft taxi, vehicles move. Scroll.

SYNTHETIC DATA The airport, airlines, tails and passengers are generated. No model is called: agents are deterministic analyzers charged simulated tokens; the gate solver is a real search.

Capacity falls at the runway and ripples outward.

A thunderstorm cell takes runway capacity down to about a quarter for 45 minutes. A deterministic model queues every movement through the reduced slots and carries the delay through each aircraft's turnaround: late inbound, late outbound, a gate still occupied. The ripple reaches the gates, then the passengers.

Specialists in parallel, a solver for the schedule.

The weather agent turns the feed into bounded capacity. Flight risk, gates, passengers and ground ops each read only their own slice. The optimizer agent never solves a schedule in prose: it calls a branch-and-bound gate solver. The synthesizer, the strongest model, reads 1,780 tokens of structured results, not the 12,990 of the window.

Three plans, one clock, a human decision.

Doing nothing strands 101 passengers. The recommended plan strands 17, at $0.43 of simulated model cost for the whole run. Compare the plans in a synchronized split view, scrub 90 minutes either side of the storm, and approve one: nothing moves until a dispatcher does.

Approve the recovery plan

Approval is the human gate: it records who approved which option, makes it the plan on the airport, hashes the dispatch order and writes the audit log (in the Docker stack). No agent can take this step.
